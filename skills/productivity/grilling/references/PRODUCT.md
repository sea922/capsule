# Product branches

Read this when the subject of the interview is whether something is worth
building: a product, a feature, or which of several ideas comes first. These
are branches for the decision tree in step 2, asked through the rounds in
step 3. Skip any branch the context already settles with evidence.

## Branches to open

| Branch | Ask | A settled answer names | Still open when the answer is |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Who | Who has this problem most acutely? | A specific person or role and what the problem costs them | A category such as "developers" or "small businesses" |
| Today | What do they do about it now, even badly? | A current workaround and its cost in time, money, or errors | "Nothing exists", which often means the problem is not painful enough to act on |
| Demand | What is the strongest evidence that someone wants this? | Behavior: paying, repeated use, a request tied to a deadline, a workflow built around a prototype | Interest: compliments, sign-ups, the size of the market |
| Narrowest version | What is the smallest version that would be useful to that person? | One workflow that could ship in days and be used as is | "It only works once the whole platform exists" |
| Not building | What is deliberately left out? | Named exclusions the user accepts | Nothing, or a list that keeps growing in the other direction |
| Success | What observation would show it worked, and by when? | A measure, a threshold, and a date, plus the result that would mean stop | A feeling, or a measure nobody can currently collect |
| Why now | What changed that makes this possible or necessary now? | A specific change in technology, cost, regulation, or the user's situation | "The market is growing" |
| Observation | What has watching a real user shown that was not expected? | A specific surprise from a session nobody steered | Surveys and demo calls that went as expected |

## How to press

Follow the challenge rules in step 4, with three product-specific habits:

- **Ask for the instance.** When an answer is general, ask for the last time it
  happened and to whom. One real case outweighs a described trend.
- **Separate evidence from belief.** Record which answers rest on something
  observed and which are the user's expectation. An expectation is a
  legitimate answer; it goes into the summary as an assumption with an owner.
- **Do not supply the evidence yourself.** Whether demand exists is a fact
  about the world. Hand a gap to the caller's `research` workflow, or propose
  the cheapest test that would close it, instead of reasoning the gap away.

## Choosing between several ideas

Have the user estimate each idea's impact, their confidence in that estimate,
and its effort, then compare. Treat the numbers as a way to expose
disagreement, not as the decision: ask what evidence sits behind each
confidence figure, and name any constraint, such as a dependency or a
deadline, that overrides the ranking.

## What goes back to the caller

Add to the summary in step 5: the person the work is for, the evidence of
demand and its strength, the narrowest version, the exclusions, the success
measure with its stop condition, and each unvalidated assumption with the test
that would check it. Whether to build stays the user's decision; state your
recommendation and the weakest branch it depends on.
